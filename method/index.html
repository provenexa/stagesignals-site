How this is built

Every night the readout is built from public reports and company filings, checked, reviewed by a person, then sent for approval before anything is published.

Stages

Each headline is placed on a ladder, from least to most concrete:

  • INTENT: announced or discussed; land, clearances or funding still open
  • APPROVED: a board or authority has approved; not yet started
  • CONTRACTED: a contract with a named counterparty is signed
  • GROUND BROKEN: a foundation stone or site start is reported
  • BUILT: commissioned or running (including trial runs)

Evidence badges

Come from our own daily ledger, which tries to match each media report to a company's exchange filing:

  • FILING MATCHED the company's own exchange filing states this, and is listed as a source
  • NO FILING MATCH the company files with the exchanges, but we found no filing that states this (that does not mean none exists)
  • MEDIA ONLY media reports only; there is no company filing to match

A match is a lead, not proof of identity or scope.

What is checked, and how

  • A fact is kept only if it has a quote that is really in the article it came from.
  • Every number and name in a written line must occur in that article.
  • Amounts are read from the quoted sentence itself.
  • Where the company has filed with the stock exchanges, the filing is read and checked against the same figures.
  • We look for other, independent outlets reporting the same event, and say how many agree on the amount.
  • "Why it matters" and "who can act" are our own reading, always labelled as such, and checked only for numbers, names, and that they contain no advice or price talk.
  • Where reports disagree, we say so, or leave the detail out rather than guess.

Limits, honestly

  • Most events are still checked against a small number of articles, not the whole press.
  • Company sector labels are not always reliable.
  • English only, for now.
  • Nothing here is investment advice, and nothing is published without a person reviewing it first.
